## 2. The app has a palette now

New: `presentation/theme/Theme.kt`. **Every raw colour in the app is `private` to that
one file.** A `Color(0xFF…)` appearing in a screen is a bug; the only legitimate
literal elsewhere is `Color.Transparent`, which is an absence of paint rather than a
choice of one.

- **One accent, electric blue**, carrying every "this is live / this is selected"
  signal: the chosen day cell, the outlined today cell, the selected tab's pill.
- **The accent swaps tone between schemes, not hue.** `#0F51D6` (tone 40) is legible
  on white and invisible on black; `#A9C4FF` (tone 80) is the reverse. Neither scheme
  is the one that "works less well".
- `tertiary` is a cyan from the same cool family rather than a contrasting hue, so a
  component that reaches for it by default cannot drag a stray colour in.
- Dark base is `#0E1116`, **not** OLED black: on true black the surface-container
  ladder is invisible and every raised surface then needs an outline to exist.
- No dynamic colour. It is Android-only (iOS would look like a different product) and
  it would hand the accent — the thing every selection state is built on — to a
  wallpaper.

### Contrast was checked before any Kotlin was written

A throwaway WCAG script over every on-colour pair. One pair failed and it changed the
design: the standard M3 pill (`primaryContainer` behind the selected nav item) is
**1.11:1** against the bar's own `surfaceContainer` in the light scheme — a selection
you cannot see. So the pill is filled with `primary` itself (**5.7:1** light, 9.8:1
dark), `selectedIconColor = onPrimary` (it sits *inside* the pill),
`selectedTextColor = primary` (it sits *below*, on the bar), unselected both
`onSurfaceVariant` at ~8:1. Weakest pair anywhere is 4.26:1, for `outline`, which
paints borders rather than glyphs.

### `GymAccents`: the colours M3 has no role for

Also in `Theme.kt`. Right now one pair — the red behind a row being swiped away.
`error` is a true red in light and a pale salmon in dark; `errorContainer` is the
reverse; and a **screen cannot choose between them, because `colorScheme` does not say
which scheme it is**. So the choice is made once, where both schemes are in view, and
handed out through `LocalGymAccents`. Screens still name no colour of their own.

### Window background (Android only)

`androidApp/src/main/res/values{,-night}/themes.xml`, wired into the manifest. The
launch window existed before Compose did and was painting stock
`Theme.Material.Light` white under a dark app, so launching in dark mode flashed. Two
values, and **they are the only duplicated colours in the project** — keep them in
step with `Theme.kt`.

## 3. Icons are drawn in `commonMain`

New: `presentation/theme/GymIcons.kt` — `Dumbbell`, `Catalogue`, `Scale`,
`ChevronRight`, `Trash`.

They replace emoji (🏋 📋 ⚖), which are font glyphs: rendered by the platform, so they
differ between Android and iOS, they **ignore the tint a navigation bar sets for a
selected item**, and they sit on whatever baseline the emoji font decides.

`material-icons-core` is not on this project's classpath and the extended set was
deprecated in Compose Multiplatform 1.7, so adding either would pull a dependency in
to draw five glyphs — and neither ships a dumbbell.

**The rules that keep them a set:** 24×24 viewport, stroked at 2 units, never filled,
round caps and joins, geometry kept 1 unit inside the viewport (a 2-wide stroke drawn
*on* the edge is clipped in half). All five go through one `gymIcon()` stencil, so
changing the weight of the whole set is one number. The declared `Color.Black` is a
stencil, not a colour choice — `Icon()` overwrites it with a theme tint.

## 4. Navigation reads its selection off a back stack

`App.kt`. The tabs were a `selectedIndex` sitting beside two `showX` booleans — two
pieces of state that can disagree, so the bar could say "Activities" while the muscle
catalogue was on screen.

Now a `Route` enum (each route knows its tab) and a saveable `List<Route>`.
`selected = destination == backStack.last().destination` — **derived, never stored**.

Still no navigation library: these routes take no arguments and have no deep links, so
a nav graph would be a dependency to model a list. Add one when a route gains either.

The stack survives process death as a list of **ordinals** — enums have no built-in
`Saver` in CMP, and storing the constants restores as null.

**Behaviour change:** selecting a tab resets that section to its root. Open Muscles →
switch to Weight → back to Log now lands on the log, not the catalogue. Deliberate:
the catalogues are edit-then-leave screens.

## 5. ⚠️ The swipe-to-delete incident — read this one

Rows on the log, activities and categories screens lost their **Delete** buttons in
favour of a left swipe revealing a red panel and a trash icon.

The first implementation used **`SwipeToDismissBox`** (material3 1.11.0-alpha07). It
misbehaved twice in one test run:

1. Mid-drag it painted the red backdrop as a **frame around a row that had not
   moved**.
2. **It deleted a workout log that was never confirmed.** The session `monday pecho`
   (Wed 19 Aug) is gone from the database. Cancel was pressed on its dialog; the row
   went anyway. I could not reconstruct the exact click path.

Best explanation, unproven: **`AnchoredDraggable` settles on velocity, not just
position.** A few-pixel flick can exceed the velocity threshold and commit the
dismissal, firing the dismiss callback with no visible gesture.

**So `SwipeToDelete` in `presentation/common/UiComponents.kt` is hand-rolled** —
`draggable` + `Animatable`, committing on **position only** (30% of width), velocity
ignored entirely. There is no dismissed state anywhere in it to reach by accident.

> If someone "simplifies" this back onto `SwipeToDismissBox`, re-read this section
> first. A control that can delete a row nobody swiped is not worth the convenience.

### The contract: the row always springs back

A drag past the threshold calls `onDelete` and then animates the row home
**regardless**. That is what lets one component serve every caller:

- a **workout log** opens a confirmation the user may cancel;
- a **category** does the same;
- an **activity** delete is *refused outright* by the domain once sessions reference it.

A row that animated itself away on release would be erasing rows still in the
database — and since the lists are fed by the database, they would flicker straight
back. **The row leaves when the data says it has gone, never because it was swiped.**

### Accessibility

A swipe is unreachable by a screen reader, so each row registers Delete as a
`CustomAccessibilityAction`. **Written but not verified** — TalkBack was never run.

## 7. The Activities strip actually filters now

It was wired to the **week** the selected day sits in, so all seven cells answered
identically: "Trained 24–30 Aug 2026" whichever one you pressed.

- `observeActivityVolume(day, day)` instead of the week range.
- Renamed throughout: `weekVolume` → `dayVolume`, `trainedThisWeek` → `trainedOnDay`,
  `WeekState` → `DayState`.
- **The strip's dots stay week-wide.** `observeSessionDays` still reads the whole
  week: those markers exist to say which *other* days hold something, and narrowed to
  the selected day they would only ever mark the cell you are standing on.
- The row's volume line dropped its day count — over a single day "1 day" is always
  true and the header already says which day.
- Four string keys renamed `*_week` → `*_day`, two reworded.

**A day with nothing trained now shows an empty state**, not the whole catalogue under
a header saying nothing was trained. The header claimed empty while the screen below
was full, and the eye believes the screen.

**The cost, deliberate:** on such a day no activity row is on screen, so editing,
archiving or deleting one means first picking a day it was trained on. Creating is
unaffected — New is in the header, which is why the empty state's hint points there.
If this bites, the natural fix is an "all days" escape in the first filter row.

## 8. DISTANCE_TIME activities are loggable — the gap is closed

The gap both previous logs called the biggest one in the app. Reported as "it says not
trained but I trained it"; the database said otherwise, and the database was right:

```
activities: (3, 'bici 20k', 'DISTANCE_TIME')   ← zero sessions
```

Three separate blocks, all removed:

1. the "New log" picker was filtered to `STRENGTH`, so the activity never appeared;
2. `StartOrResumeSessionUseCase` returns `NotAStrengthActivity` for anything else;
3. **the schema had nowhere to put the numbers.**

### Schema 3 → 4 (`3.sqm`)

Two nullable columns on `workout_session`: `distance_meters`, `duration_seconds`.

**Units are never stored — metres and seconds, always.** What the user types (km,
minutes) and what they read back are presentation, so moving to miles later is a
formatting change rather than a migration. Integers for the same reason a set count is
one: 20.5 km is exactly 20500 m, and no rounding drifts across a save/load.

Both null for every strength session, so it is additive in effect as well as in form.

### `LogDistanceTimeUseCase`

Deliberately **not** folded into `StartOrResumeSessionUseCase`. That one opens a
session and leaves it open for muscles to be checked into over a day; this one has
everything at the moment it is called. A shared entry point would mean a "start" that
sometimes finishes and sometimes does not.

It still routes through `startOrResumeSession`, so logging the same activity twice on
one day **corrects** that day rather than stacking a second row — matching strength.

Either measure alone is accepted (a swim not measured, a route not timed); an empty
form is refused, and **zero is refused too** — it reads as "measured, and it was
none", which is never what was meant.

### The rest of the flow

- The picker lists **every** activity; the measurement type decides which form opens.
  Splitting into two buttons would make the user classify the activity before picking
  it, which the app already knows.
- The dialog parses text **on submit**, not per keystroke: `"20."` has to be passable
  on the way to `"20.5"`, and the comma is folded to a point because it is the decimal
  separator in three of the four locales.
- Reading back: `20.5 km · 45 min · 2:12 /km`. Tapping the row reopens the form
  prefilled, which is also how it gets corrected.
- The Activities row shows km instead of sets, **driven by what was measured, not by
  the activity's declared type** — a session logged before a type change still reads
  correctly.
- `formatMeasure()` in `UiComponents.kt` drops a trailing `.0`: the user typed 45
  minutes, and "45.0 min" claims a precision they never gave.

**Ten new tests** (`DistanceTimeLoggingTest`, plus two migration tests): metres/seconds
round-trip, counts as trained by the exact query the screen filters on, strength
refused with nothing written, empty form refused, one measure alone, same-day
correction, pace arithmetic, another day does not leak in.

**The migration was verified against the real device database**, not just in-memory:
`user_version: 4`, both columns present, every activity, session and muscle entry
intact.

## 10. Verification notes — what cost time

Everything above was checked on `emulator-5554` (1080×2424 @ 420dpi), not just
compiled. Re-read §6 of the 2026-08-24 log first; all of it still applies. New this
session:

- **Wait for the app to settle before tapping.** Several early failures came from
  firing `input tap` immediately after `am start` or after a theme change: the
  coordinates were from the previous layout, and taps landed on whatever now occupied
  that spot — a date-picker opened where a week arrow should have been. Dump the view
  tree first; the dump itself is the delay.
- **`adb shell cat` corrupts a SQLite file** exactly as it corrupts a PNG. Use
  `adb exec-out "run-as <pkg> cat databases/gym.db"` — pulling the database and
  querying it with Python is the only way to tell a UI bug from a data bug, and it is
  what settled both §5 and §8.
- **`KEYCODE_BACK` exits the app**, it does not just dismiss a dialog.
- **A heredoc containing apostrophes intermittently breaks the Bash tool.** Write the
  script to the scratchpad with the Write tool and run it by path instead.
- **Screenshotting a gesture**: one on-device shell doing
  `input swipe … & sleep 0.8; screencap -p /sdcard/x.png`, then pull. The `&` is what
  lets the capture happen mid-drag.
